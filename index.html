Jonathan Basile’s digital realization of Jorge Luis Borges’ The Library of Babel demonstrates an algorithmic system that is exhaustive, indifferent, and fundamentally non-semantic. It generates every possible permutation of language without understanding, intention, or truth. Meaning emerges only when a human encounters the output and attempts to interpret it.

Large language models operate on a parallel logic. Trained on vast corpora of text, they do not know or reason in any human sense, but statistically predict what language should come next based on prior patterns. Like the Library, they can produce passages that appear coherent, insightful, or profound—alongside infinite noise, contradiction, and error—without any internal distinction between the two.

Hallucinations positions generative AI not as an oracle, but as an algorithmic mirror: capable of producing convincing language at scale, while remaining structurally indifferent to meaning, accuracy, or intent. This project functions as both an educational and demonstrative space, designed to surface the mechanics, affordances, and limitations of contemporary language models through form, interaction, and repetition.

By situating AI output within a Borges-inspired framework of combinatorics and infinity, the project emphasizes a critical truth: generative systems do not create meaning—they enumerate possibilities. Interpretation, judgment, and responsibility remain external, human acts.
